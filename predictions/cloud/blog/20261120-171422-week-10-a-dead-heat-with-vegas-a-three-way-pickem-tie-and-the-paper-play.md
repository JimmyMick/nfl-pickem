Week 9 made the model look like it had football solved (14-1). Week 10 was the regression-to-the-mean chaser: a clean, unspectacular **9-4 straight up** — the exact same record as the market, off the exact same picks. No genius, no disaster. Just a quiet week where the model and Vegas saw the slate identically and four games refused to cooperate with either of them.

## The model and the market went stride for stride

Thirteen games, and the model and the market picked the **same side in all thirteen**. Both finished **9-4 (69%)**. When you and Vegas agree on every single game, you're going to live and die together — and this week you both died four times:

- **Carolina over New Orleans** — model and market both leaned NO (narrowly), CAR took it.
- **Cleveland over Houston** — both had HOU, the Browns spoiled it.
- **Minnesota over Green Bay** — both liked GB at ~58%, Vikings pulled the road upset.
- **Washington over NY Giants** — both had NYG at ~55%, WAS won it.

Three of those four were genuine coin-flip-ish games the model never had strong conviction on, so the calibration doesn't take much of a hit. The one that stings a little is **GB over MIN at home** — that was the model's firmest wrong call of the day.

Where it earned its keep: the model (and market) read the road dogs correctly — **Buffalo at the Jets, Jacksonville at Tennessee, LA over Arizona, Seattle at Las Vegas** all came in as called, even though the model was backing the *away* favorite against a soft home number in most of them. On a slate with four upsets, hitting 9 of 13 and matching the sharpest line in the building is a perfectly respectable day.

Season-to-date straight up: **model 97-148... er, 97 of 148 (66%), market 101 of 148 (68%).** Two games separate them over ten weeks. That gap *is* the thesis of this whole project — the market is a hair sharper, and we're not pretending otherwise.
