Week 9 is in the books, and if you only looked at one number you'd think the model solved football: **14-1 straight up (93%)**. That's its best week of the season by a mile. But the fun stuff is underneath — the AI and CMac just lit up the human leaderboard, JMack's lead evaporated, and the paper play took *another* one on the chin. Let's get into it.

## The model had its best week — and so did everyone else

Fourteen of fifteen. The only miss was **Green Bay at New England**, where GB won and both the model *and* the market had it the other way. That's the tell for the whole week: this was a **chalk week**, and the model tied the market straight-up at 14-1.

So before anyone frames the model a genius, read the calibration: the market was still the sharper thermometer — **log loss 0.417 to the model's 0.515**, Brier 0.121 to 0.164. On a week where almost everything went according to form, Vegas priced it a touch tighter. The model matched the market on *who won*; the market still edged it on *how sure to be*. That's exactly the honest place a market-grade model lives.

Credit where it's due, though: the model nailed **Dallas at Indianapolis** (it liked the Cowboys by +13% over the market — Dallas delivered) and **Jacksonville at Baltimore**, where it had the Jaguars live as a big road dog and JAX pulled the upset. Reading those right is the whole point.

## The paper play still can't catch a break

Here's the cruel irony. The model's biggest single disagreement with the market — the paper play — landed on **Baltimore (home) at +380** against Jacksonville. The model had that game near a coin flip while the market buried the Ravens. **Jacksonville won.** Another **–10 units**, moneyline *and* spread both red.

That drops the moneyline paper play to a genuinely ugly **1-8 on the season (–65.5u)**. The thesis — blindly back the single biggest model-vs-market gap — is getting a brutal real-world stress test, and it's failing it. "Biggest disagreement" keeps proving it is *not* a synonym for "best bet."

The quieter sibling tells a better story, though: the **spread** version of the paper play is **31-25 (+31.8u)** on the year. Same picks, graded against the number instead of the moneyline — and it's actually green. The lesson keeps repeating: the model's edge, where it has one, shows up against the spread, not chasing dog moneylines.
