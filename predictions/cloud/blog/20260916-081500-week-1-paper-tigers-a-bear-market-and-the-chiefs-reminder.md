Week 1 is in the books, and if you were riding the favorites, you had a good Sunday. The **market went 12-4 (75%)** straight up; our **model went 10-6 (62%)**. Chalk mostly held — which is exactly the kind of week where the model, built to be *calibrated* rather than contrarian, tips its cap to Vegas.

Our headline contrarian swing — the paper play on **Miami** as the week's biggest model-vs-market disagreement (+18% on the Dolphins) — **whiffed**; Vegas was right on that one. But the signature result of the week was **San Francisco walking into L.A. as a +180 dog and winning by 20.** Which brings us to the three questions everyone's asking.

## Are the Rams paper tigers?

Let's not bury it: **SF 27, Rams 7 — a virtual home game, as a -218 favorite.** That's not a bad beat, that's a faceplant.

Here's what makes it spicy. Vegas has the Rams as the **outright Super Bowl favorite** — de-vigged, about a **12.6%** chance to win it all, #1 on the board. Our model? It's been the skeptic in the room all offseason, giving L.A. roughly a **3.8%** title shot, ~60% to even make the playoffs, and just 16% to win their own division (Seattle and the NFC West are a buzzsaw).

One game doesn't make a team a paper tiger. But when the market's *anointed* team gets housed at home in Week 1, and your model was already pricing them a third of where Vegas was — the gap suddenly looks less like the model being stubborn and more like the model seeing something. **Verdict: not paper tigers yet, but the burden of proof just shifted to the Rams.**

## Are the Bears ready to make a run?

**Chicago 59, Carolina 37.** Fifty-nine points. The offense didn't just show up, it threw a party.

The model liked the Bears here (so did the market), so no surprise on the *W* — but read the whole line: **59-37.** They also gave up 37. That's a shootout, not a statement of dominance. The season sim has Chicago at ~9.4 projected wins, **53% to make the playoffs**, 23% to win the division — a fun, frisky wild-card-bubble team, not a juggernaut. Vegas has their title odds at 3.3%; we're at 2.7%.

**Verdict: the offense looks real and appointment-viewing, but "make a run" means a playoff push, not a parade — until the defense proves it can get a stop.**

## Are the Chiefs back for another Super Bowl run?

Here's the fun part: **our own model doubted them.** Kansas City opened as a *home underdog in our numbers* — the model gave K.C. only 40% against Denver. Then the Chiefs went out and won **31-10.** The machine ate crow, and deserved it.

Longer term, the model still respects the résumé where it counts: **~10.2 projected wins, 77.5% to make the playoffs, and 59% to win the AFC West** — the highest division number of these three teams by a mile. The Super Bowl number is measured (3.3% for us, ~4.8% at Vegas), because a title is always a long shot for anyone. But the division? The model says believe it.

**Verdict: don't overreact to one game either way — but the Chiefs answered the doubt emphatically, and they remain the team to beat in their division.**
