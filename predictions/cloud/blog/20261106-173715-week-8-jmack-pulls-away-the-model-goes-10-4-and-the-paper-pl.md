Week 8 is in the books, and it was a good one for the model — **10-4 straight up (71%)**, its best week in a while. But the headline in our little contest isn't the model. It's **JMack**, who put up an 11-3 week and turned a tight race into a lead. Let's break it down.

## The model had a week

Ten of fourteen. After a few middling weeks where the market kept nudging ahead, the model came out of Week 8 looking sharp. The misses were the kind you live with:

- **Arizona over Dallas** — we had the Cowboys, the Cardinals didn't care. A straight-up upset against both us and the market.
- **Chicago over Seattle** — the Bears keep making us look silly. We leaned Seattle; Chicago won anyway. (This is becoming a theme with Chicago — forecaster, not oracle.)
- **Las Vegas over the Jets** and **Atlanta/Tampa** rounding out the four Ls.

Where it mattered, though, the model threaded some needles. Its biggest disagreement with the market where it *backed the home side* was **Baltimore–Buffalo**: the model had Buffalo's spot better than the market did, and **Buffalo delivered**. Season-long calibration stays dead even with Vegas — log loss 0.644 to the market's 0.648, Brier tied at 0.227. Exactly where a market-grade model should live: not beating the line, but reading it honestly.

## The paper play takes its first real gut-punch

For weeks the single-biggest-disagreement paper play had been quietly stacking chips. Week 8 is where it blinked.

The play was **Philadelphia at Washington** — the model had the Eagles as a live dog (54% at home-equivalent) while the market buried them. We took Philly at **+190**. Washington won. **–10 units**, and the week's boldest contrarian call went down with it.

That's the deal with chasing the biggest gap on the board: when the model is right about a mispriced dog, it pays like a dog. When it's wrong, it's wrong by a lot. One loss doesn't retire the thesis — but it's a reminder that "biggest disagreement" and "best bet" aren't the same sentence.
